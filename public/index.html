Interactive Diffusion Policy: Safe Reactive Avoidance through Proximity Sensing in Human-Robot Shared Environments

Robotics Innovatory · Department of Mechanical Engineering · Sungkyunkwan University
IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L)
Website source

Task execution, reactive avoidance, and recovery data collection on the RB10.

Proximity sensing enables local avoidance. Recovery-aware latent guidance helps the robot resume the task afterward.

Abstract

A manipulator sharing its workspace with people must avoid approaching obstacles and complete its task afterward. Reactive avoidance can move the robot outside the demonstration distribution, where a learned policy struggles to resume. Visual occlusion and transparent objects also challenge camera-based obstacle detection.

Interactive Diffusion Policy (IDP) connects task execution, reactive avoidance, and recovery. Diffusion Policy generates reference targets from RGB and proprioceptive observations. A continuously running RMPflow controller combines target tracking with avoidance driven by robot-mounted capacitive proximity sensing. Latent Policy Barrier (LPB) guides task resumption using a dynamics model trained on recovery trajectories collected after actual RMPflow deflections, with human guidance when the policy cannot continue.

Sensor experiments examine occlusion and transparent glass. Manipulation experiments compare recovery data and controller configurations, with Recovery-aware LPB reaching 91.3% task completion from post-avoidance states.

From Task Execution to Task Resumption

Diffusion Policy

RGB observations and proprioception condition an action chunk that supplies reference targets for the task.

RMPflow avoidance

The low-level controller continuously combines reference tracking with proximity-driven obstacle avoidance.

LPB recovery

When the latent OOD cost rises above its threshold, learned dynamics guides action generation toward the expert distribution.

Proximity measurements feed RMPflow. LPB responds to the observation's distance from the expert latent set. The controller therefore reacts to an approaching obstacle while the policy receives recovery guidance when avoidance leaves it outside its learned distribution.

Proximity Sensing Around the Robot

Link-mounted sensors provide local distance information when an external camera cannot see an approaching object.

Learning from Avoidance and Recovery

Collect the transitions the robot encounters after its avoidance controller deflects it from the task.

  1. Run the policy

    An intermediate checkpoint executes the pick-and-place task. An obstacle approaches during the rollout.

  2. Avoid, then guide

    RMPflow produces the avoidance motion. When the policy cannot resume, the operator provides human guidance until execution continues.

  3. Train the dynamics

    Fixed-horizon transitions from expert demonstrations and recovery rollouts train LPB's action-conditioned latent dynamics.

100 expert demonstrations train the base policy, define the expert latent reference, and provide dynamics-training samples.

Recovery trajectories contain policy rollout, avoidance, and human-guided resumption, expanding dynamics coverage around avoidance-induced states.

Real-Robot Experiments

Wide-range pick-and-place with a Rainbow Robotics RB10, RGB cameras, a parallel-jaw gripper, and link-mounted proximity sensors.
